01 · The track

Every account gets its own lane.

Each one is a separate Claude with its own login, chats, settings and colored icon. They all run at once, and nothing leaks between them.

02 · The clock

Watch the track, not the clock.

5-hour and weekly limits for every lane, live. The small mark is the pace marker: a bar that ends left of it is burning slower than the clock. Cross 80% and you get a heads-up.

03 · Most room

Always know where there’s room.

The lane with the most headroom floats to the top of the menu bar. One click, or ⌥⌘B from anywhere, and you’re in it.

04 · Reset

And the minute it resets, you’ll know.

The alert is scheduled with macOS, so it lands right on time even if Lanes has quit. Back on the lane you love.

No account. No telemetry.

Usage is read with each profile’s own session, straight from claude.ai. Nothing else leaves your Mac.

~6,000lines of Swift and shell you can read in an afternoon.

  • A health check. Settings → Status spots what’s off and fixes it in one click.
  • Hands-off updates. A watchdog puts each lane back on its account after Claude updates.
  • Sign-in that lands right. Claude’s login link goes to the lane you clicked it in.

In the terminal

Every lane in your terminal.

Each lane’s Code tab already runs Claude Code on its own account. lanes claude brings that account to your terminal, with the same sign-in, settings and history. Your plain claude stays on Default.

  • lanes statusline onEach lane’s usage in Claude Code’s status line, as in the window here.
  • lanes usage --jsonEvery lane’s numbers, for scripts, prompts and tmux.
  • lanes doctor --fixChecks the setup and repairs what it can.

Get on the track.

  1. Paste the command in Terminal. Lanes lands in Applications and opens in your menu bar.
  2. New Profile, open it, sign in. That’s a lane.
  3. That’s it. Lanes installs its own updates; your profiles stay as they are.
$ curl -fsSL https://lanes.pego.tech/install | sh

Prefer a download? Get the .dmg. It isn’t notarized by Apple, so the first launch needs
System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. The command above skips that, and you can read it first.
